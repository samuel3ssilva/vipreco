UX / Product Strategy Review · Pilot Readiness

ViPreço MVP — Complete Assessment

Five screens reviewed: Home/Achados · Search · Comparison · Deal Detail · WhatsApp Opt-in. Audiences: end customer (B2C) and supermarket owner (B2B). Scope: pre-pilot, Artemis region.

1. Executive Summary

The MVP is unusually disciplined for this stage: one job per screen, a coherent green/yellow identity, and transparency (source, collection date, validity) present on every surface. The Home → Search → Compare → Detail → WhatsApp journey is logical and the CTA language ("Comparar em 3 mercados") is consistent and predictable. WhatsApp as the retention channel is the right call for the Brazilian market.

The core problem: the app shows prices but never shows savings. Nowhere does a user see "you save R$ 1,50" — the single number that makes the value proposition land instantly. Second: trust is communicated with repeated small grey text instead of a compact visual badge system, so the app's biggest differentiator reads as clutter. Third: "mercados próximos" is claimed but never proven — no neighborhood selector, no distance on any store. Fourth: the 5-tab navigation promises destinations ("Comparar", "Favoritos") the MVP doesn't support as standalone flows. Fifth: there is nothing on screen a supermarket owner would recognize as being for them.

Key strengths
  • Transparency metadata on every screen — rare and valuable
  • Exact-product focus avoids the "generic match" credibility trap
  • Predictable, repeated CTA pattern; low learning cost
  • WhatsApp opt-in is benefit-led, with an explicit "no spam" promise
  • Comparison screen ranks cheapest-first with honest disclaimers
Biggest bottlenecks
  • Savings never quantified — prices without deltas
  • Trust expressed as repeated text, not a badge system
  • No location context (neighborhood, distance, radius)
  • Nav overpromises: 2 real flows, 5 tabs
  • Zero visible B2B value; merchants see only a race to the bottom

2. Screen-by-Screen Breakdown

Screen 1 — Home / Achados

What works: "Destaque do dia" is the right anchor; the search placeholder ("arroz, café, leite…") teaches usage; one hero card + a short list is honest for MVP inventory.

  • Add a location anchor at the top. "Achados de Artemis" is a headline, not a control. Make it a tappable neighborhood selector with a pin icon — it proves "nearby" and prepares multi-region expansion.
  • Two competing green CTAs. "Comparar em 3 mercados" (hero) and "Receber achados no WhatsApp" fight for primacy. Demote WhatsApp to an outline/ghost button or a dismissible banner; one filled primary per screen.
  • Hero card hierarchy: price (R$ 17,99) should be the largest element after the product name; the source/validity lines should collapse into two compact chips ("Comunidade · 24/05" and "Válido até 27/05"). Add the missing number: "R$ 1,50 mais barato que o 2º mercado."
  • "Outros achados" rows are inert: no savings signal, no store distance, no tap affordance. Add a right-aligned green delta ("−R$ 2,10") and a chevron.
  • Missing for MVP: quick-search category chips under the search bar (café, arroz, leite) — cheap to build, removes typing for the top-5 queries.

Screen 2 — Search Results

What works: exact-product results with brand, size, unit price and best observed price; the "suggest a product" fallback turns dead ends into data collection.

  • "Menor preço observado" repeats on every card — it's a column header pretending to be body text. Say it once above the list ("Mostrando o menor preço observado em cada produto") and free three lines of vertical space.
  • Three stacked full-width dark buttons are heavy. Make the whole card tappable (it's the obvious expectation) and reduce the CTA to a text button with chevron: "Comparar em 3 mercados ›".
  • No sort/filter. Even a single toggle — "por preço / por preço por kg" — matters here: R$ 17,99 (35,98/kg) vs R$ 18,49 (36,98/kg) is exactly the comparison unit price exists for.
  • The "Não encontrou?" module is good but passive. It reads as a footnote. Give it a real button ("Sugerir produto") and keep the container light grey — it should never outweigh a result card. Its placement at list end is correct; don't move it up.

Screen 3 — Product Comparison

What works: the strongest screen. Cheapest-first ranking with numbered positions, per-store metadata, promo conditions ("Limite de 2 por cliente") and an honest disclaimer.

  • Crown the winner. Row #1 should be visually distinct: green border/fill, a "Melhor preço" tag, and the delta that sells the app — "R$ 1,50 mais barato que o 2º". Right now #1 and #3 have identical visual weight.
  • Do the promo math for the user. "Leve 3, pague 2" on a R$ 17,99 item means an effective R$ 11,99/unit — that computed number should appear on the chip ("Leve 3, pague 2 → R$ 11,99/un"). Multi-buy promos scan as noise until the app translates them into a price.
  • Metadata is tripled. Fonte + data + validade repeat as two icon-lines per store. Compress to a single line per row ("Comunidade · 24/05 · até 27/05") and reserve the expanded version for the detail screen.
  • Add distance per store ("1,2 km") — it's the second variable in every real decision, and it's the time-saving half of the value proposition.
  • The "Importante" disclaimer is correct but shouts (title + filled box). One quiet grey line at list end retains honesty without undermining confidence.

Screen 4 — Deal Detail

What works: strike-through previous price; the "Confiança da informação" table is the product's signature idea and belongs here.

  • "Abrir no WhatsApp" is semantically ambiguous — open what? A chat with whom? If it sends the deal to the user's own WhatsApp, label the outcome: "Enviar oferta para meu WhatsApp". If the primary job here is "go buy it", the primary CTA should be "Como chegar" (map/route), with WhatsApp and Compartilhar as secondary. A detail screen whose main action is a messaging app suggests the app doesn't believe in its own next step.
  • Turn the trust table into a trust loop. Add "Confirmado por 12 pessoas esta semana" plus two micro-actions: "✓ Confirmei este preço" and "Reportar preço diferente". This converts passive metadata into community validation — and generates the freshness data the whole product depends on.
  • Show the saving, twice: vs the previous price ("−12% · você economiza R$ 2,50") and vs the most expensive nearby market. The strike-through alone makes the user do arithmetic.
  • Missing, high-value: a 30-day price sparkline (even 4 data points) and "Avise-me se baixar" — both directly monetize the data you're already collecting and give users a reason to return.

Screen 5 — WhatsApp Opt-in

What works: benefit-led structure, explicit "sem spam" and "pode sair quando quiser" — good consent design.

  • Trigger it contextually, not as a destination. The screen converts best after a moment of demonstrated value — right after a comparison or a saved deal ("Quer receber achados como este?").
  • Add frequency and scope controls (daily/weekly; my neighborhood only) — one extra row that dramatically reduces churn from the channel.
  • Add social proof: "2.300 vizinhos de Artemis já recebem" — the single most effective element on opt-in screens; even a modest true number works.
  • The confetti/percent decorations around the WhatsApp logo read as clip-art — and oversized third-party logo use carries brand-guideline risk. A small monochrome glyph next to your own headline is safer and looks more professional.

3. Cross-Cutting Analysis

Global UX & Navigation

The core journey is logical and short — good. The structural issue is the bottom bar: "Comparar" is not a destination (comparison is always contextual to a product; a global tab lands nowhere sensible) and "Favoritos" has no visible supporting flow in the MVP. Ship three tabs — Achados · Buscar · Lista (or Favoritos, if lists slip) — and grow later. A shopping list is the highest-leverage missing feature: it aggregates deals into a basket total per store, which is where "how much do I save this week?" becomes real.

UI / Design System

The green/yellow palette is appropriate and ownable, but under-systematized. Recommend explicit tokens: green = action + best price (filled CTAs, winner rows, savings deltas); yellow = highlight + promo ("Destaque do dia", multi-buy chips); grey scale strictly for metadata. Today too many elements sit at the same small-grey level, so nothing pops. Typography needs a firmer scale: price XL/bold, product name L/semibold, unit price S, metadata XS chips. Cards need more internal padding and fewer hairlines — let whitespace do the separation. Audit icons for a single family/weight; the current mix (clock, calendar, checkmarks) varies in style.

B2C Value — Making Savings Visceral

  • A green savings delta on every price ("−R$ 1,50 vs 2º lugar") — the one change that touches all five screens.
  • Translate every multi-buy promo into an effective unit price, automatically.
  • Distance chips make the time saving tangible; a running "você já economizou R$ 34 este mês" tally (post-pilot) makes it habitual.

B2B Value — From Threat to Channel

As designed, a merchant sees only a leaderboard that punishes anyone who isn't #1 — a race to the bottom. Three UI moves reframe it:

  • "Parceiro oficial" badge: merchants who supply official prices get a verified badge and richer presence (logo, hours, address). This is the recruitment pitch in pixel form — join and your data is authoritative, not crowdsourced about you.
  • Promos as differentiation: the comparison already surfaces "Leve 3, pague 2" — lean in. A store that isn't cheapest per unit can still win the row via promo mechanics, loyalty perks, or bundle deals. That's the anti-race-to-the-bottom story to tell owners.
  • Measurable foot traffic: "Como chegar" taps, deal shares, and price confirmations per store are countable events. Even a simple monthly WhatsApp report to merchants — "sua loja apareceu em 1.400 comparações, 230 pessoas traçaram rota" — is the B2B retention product. Instrument these events now; a labeled "Oferta patrocinada" slot becomes the revenue line later.

Trust & Data Validation

"Coleta da comunidade" is honest but, repeated as plain text, it reads as "unverified". Replace it with a three-tier visual badge system, used identically everywhere:

Tier Badge Meaning
1 Comunidade Single community report, with date
2 ✓ Confirmado ×12 Corroborated by multiple users this week
3 Oficial do mercado Supplied/validated by the merchant (B2B hook)

Pair it with color-coded freshness (green ≤48h, amber ≤5 days, grey older) and the confirm/report loop from Screen 4. The tier system does triple duty: cleaner UI, stronger consumer trust, and a concrete reason for merchants to join (tier 3 is only reachable by partnering).

4. Action Plan — Top 5 Before Pilot

# Recommendation Impact Effort
1 Quantify savings everywhere. Green delta vs next-cheapest on hero, results, comparison winner, and detail; auto-computed effective unit price on every multi-buy promo. Very high Low
2 Ship the trust-badge system + confirm/report loop. Replaces repeated metadata text, adds freshness color-coding, and starts the community validation flywheel. Very high Medium
3 Add location truth. Neighborhood selector on Home; distance chip on every store mention; "Como chegar" as the detail screen's primary CTA (WhatsApp secondary, relabeled by outcome). High Medium
4 Simplify navigation and CTA hierarchy. Three tabs (Achados · Buscar · Lista); one filled primary CTA per screen; winner row highlighted on comparison; lighter result cards (whole card tappable). High Low
5 Seed B2B value now. "Parceiro oficial" badge (= trust tier 3), promo mechanics as a visible way to win without the lowest price, and event instrumentation (route taps, shares, confirmations) so pilot merchants get a traffic report. High Medium

Deferred (post-pilot, deliberately): shopping list with per-store basket totals, price-history sparklines and drop alerts, monthly savings tally, sponsored placements, loyalty/coupon integrations. All depend on data the instrumented pilot will generate.
